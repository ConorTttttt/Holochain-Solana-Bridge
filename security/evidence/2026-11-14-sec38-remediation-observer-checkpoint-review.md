## Complete history and refund-state STOP conditions

The original 14106778660 ZIP proves **selected original containing blocks**, not full `getBlocks` produced-slot enumeration from deployment, all intervening block bodies, a second archival provider's independent history, or absence of pruning. OBS `indexFinalizedRange` does check two distinct RPC URLs, genesis, `getFirstAvailableBlock`, finalized tip, ordered `getBlocks` lists, every produced block's full byte-identical data, chained `parentSlot/previousBlockhash`, all signed entries and monotonic durable checkpoints. These are valuable **two matching RPC claims**, not independent consensus: both providers could collude, share a compromised backend or prune requested data. Persist and attest start/tip/gap coverage and every provider response; refuse fork, missing slot/body, ALT-table ambiguity, history gap or provider disagreement. Do not advance checkpoint while history cannot be reconstructed.

`observer/src/transaction.mjs:192-196` intentionally **throws** on an included successful bridge `refund`: “refund lifecycle needs separate reconciliation.” `observer/src/ledger.mjs` currently persists original deposit evidence, monotonic local checkpoint and occurrence uniqueness but **no fully implemented persisted refund transition**. Full historical refund coverage, restart/reconciliation and original-receipt liabilities remain unproven. Required future state machine: `UNSEEN -> OBSERVED_REFUNDABLE -> REFUNDED` (with optional non-authoritative pending state). A terminal refund must link original genesis/program/mint/receipt PDA/depositor/nonce/signed original creation occurrence and **exact original amount**, unique included refund occurrence, verified transfer and changed custody/receipt state. Reject nonce reuse, amount mismatch, second refund, backwards transition, orphan/refork, rollback of refunded state and post-restart stale credit. A refundable receipt may **never** be marked spendable. On missing history or unclear finality quarantine, not silently skip or reconstruct a favorable state.

Existing OBS `ledger.mjs` uses fsync+rename+directory fsync and an on-host checksum. A host able to rewrite both the journal and checksum can roll back or replay; two isolated journals can process the same global receipt. These properties need independent off-host evidence.
